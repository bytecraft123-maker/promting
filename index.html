01 · Introduction to Prompt Engineering

A language model produces a continuation of the information it receives. Prompt engineering is the practice of making that continuation useful, relevant, and reliable.

The Central Question

Imagine giving a language model the opening words: "The most important discovery of the experiment was…" The words surrounding the request determine which kind of continuation is likely. Without prompt engineering, it might write a sci-fi novel, a satire, or a legal summary.

Anatomy of a Well-Designed Prompt

1. Target Audience

Explicitly states who the response is for (e.g., a 12-year-old vs. a senior software architect).

2. Scope & Sequence

Supplies exact bounds on what topics to cover and in what operational order.

3. Presentation Format

Specifies structured outputs like JSON schemas, Markdown tables, or bullet points.

4. Explicit Constraints

Sets strict boundaries on length, forbidden jargon, and hallucination safeguards.

Tokenization & Budget

LLMs process numerical token IDs rather than raw characters. Punctuation, trailing spaces, and non-English scripts often require multiple tokens per word, consuming context window capacity rapidly.

Hallucination Mechanics

Hallucinations stem from next-token probability optimization. The model generates fluent, authoritative syntax even when facts or citations do not exist in its weights.

05 · Prompt Content, Context & RAG

Lexical Search (BM25 / Jaccard)

Exact keyword matching. Perfect for error codes, specific user IDs, and exact product names. Highly transparent and low latency.

Neural Vector Search (Cosine Similarity)

Converts text into dense vector embeddings. Matches semantic meanings and paraphrases across different words using angle similarity formulas.
